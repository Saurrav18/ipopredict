How it works

what this system is, validated numbers, and what it cannot do

What it does

In plain terms: several different prediction models vote on each IPO, and we only trust the call when they agree. We re-check our accuracy every week. The technical details:

An ensemble of four models (RandomForest, XGBoost, LightGBM, ExtraTrees) scores every closing mainboard IPO from live grey-market and subscription data. A calibration agent re-tunes the accuracy tiers weekly. Every claim below was measured walk-forward: models only ever see IPOs that came before the ones they predict.

Choosing your tier: volume vs accuracy

Where you set the slider is the win rate you get. It is a strategy choice, not just a safety dial. Measured on 165 IPOs the model never trained on:

SliderYou hear aboutWin rateAvg gain / pick
70%161 of 165 (98%)70%+15.2%
80%117 of 165 (71%)80%+21.0%
90%82 of 165 (50%)90%+29.3%
95%73 of 165 (44%)96%+32.6%

The trade-off in real money, putting about Rs 15,000 into each pick across these 165 IPOs: at 70% you take 161 picks, deploy roughly Rs 24 lakh, and profit about Rs 3.7 lakh - a 15% return on the money you put in. At 90% you take just 82 picks, deploy about Rs 12 lakh, and profit about Rs 3.6 lakh - nearly the same profit from half the capital, a 29% return. So a low bar reaches more winners and a little more total profit; a high bar is far more efficient per rupee with fewer misses. Note that heavily oversubscribed IPOs allot by lottery, so you may not get every pick. All figures are listing-day gains, not long-term returns.

Honest limitations

Prediction ranges hold about 88% of the time on recommended IPOs and 75% across all IPOs, not 100%. Listing gains in this market span -25% to +140%; tighter intervals would be dishonest. High accuracy tiers (95-98%) rest on roughly 40-50 historical picks each, so every new listing is an out-of-sample test and is recorded above, hit or miss. Confidence is the model's own calibrated probability that its call is right: at 90+ it has been correct 95-100% of the time, below 60 it is close to a coin flip and says so.

Before you turn on alerts

First, the honest part

IPOPredict is a prediction bot, not a financial advisor. It studies how hundreds of past IPOs behaved and estimates whether a new one is likely to list above its offer price.

It can be wrong. Nothing here is investment advice - the decision, and the money, are always yours.

How it works · 1 of 3

Your accuracy tier

The slider sets how strict your alerts are. It is a historical hit rate: at the 65% tier, about 65 of every 100 IPOs that cleared it went on to list above their offer price.

Example: set it to 90 and you only hear about the strongest IPOs (about 9 in 10 listed up historically). Set it to 65 and you hear about many more - but with more misses.

How it works · 2 of 3

When we message you

Only on an IPO's closing day, and only if it clears your tier. You get up to three short updates:

Morning read (~9:30 AM) · Midday update (~1:30 PM) · Final read (~3 PM), in time to approve your UPI mandate before the 5 PM cutoff.

No daily digests. No spam.

How it works · 3 of 3

What it is built on

The model learns from hundreds of past mainboard IPOs, and it grows every time a new one lists. More history makes it sharper.

But past results never guarantee the future. Grey market and subscription numbers can swing right up to closing, so treat every call as an estimate.
